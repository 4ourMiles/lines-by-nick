## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** Authored for this studio where it counts: Nick's handwriting is the nav, the hero glyph field, the merge plate and the footer; the three-line brand sentence with "Lines by Nick." on a coral printed block, the cobalt/coral seam and the numbered captions all belong to one world. Strip the copy and glyphs, though, and the skeleton is the agency scaffold the direction contract claims to refuse: hero, eight icon-title-blurb-tag cells, three-step strip, CTA. The isometric objects are competent but pack-generic; they would sit unchanged on any dev shop's site. Specificity lives in the handwriting, the color commitment and the copy, not yet in the structure or the drawings.

## Overall Impression

The world landed and the first viewport is recognisable at thumbnail size. The problem is the middle: 1,470px of near-identical service cells on desktop and roughly 3,000px on a phone, where the two-stream idea the page is built on becomes an alternating card feed. The single biggest opportunity is to make the service passage carry the thesis instead of restating it, and to give the one click that matters a reason to believe.

## What's Working

- **The first viewport.** "Lines by Nick." on a coral block with its coral-deep pass, the handwritten brace, semicolon and star field, and the cobalt/coral pair peeking under the fold. Thin hand against heavy outline, delivered in one scroll.
- **The mint merge cell.** The `<n>` plate, "One line, start to finish." and the three facts (Who / Price / Replies) land reassurance exactly where a buyer asks "what's the catch". The most persuasive 400px on the page.
- **Control language.** Buttons rest at a 4px pass, hover extends to 6px, press collapses, focus draws a 3px ring; the same block grammar carries nav, hero, close and the phone menu. Verified in the browser, not just in CSS.

## Priority Issues

- **[P1] Three routes, no distinction.** Hero shows "Start a project" and "Contact" at near-equal weight with no cue; the close swaps Contact for a mailto; the footer lists all three. *Why it matters:* product principle 4 is one obvious way to get in touch; a first-timer hesitates at the only click that counts. *Fix:* keep the coral button, add one plain line under it ("A few lines is enough. Plan and price within a few days, no commitment. Or email linesbynick@gmail.com."), drop the hero Contact button, keep /contact in the nav, use one verb everywhere. *Command:* /impeccable clarify.
- **[P1] Phone collapses the two streams.** Below 900px cells interleave 01.1, 02.1, 01.2, 02.2 and the tone colors (yellow, mint, coral) look random. *Why:* phone is the primary operating context and the thesis is invisible on it. *Fix:* stack the four code cells under a cobalt header, then the four ink cells under a coral header (DESIGN.md's own layout rule), or color every cap by stream. *Command:* /impeccable adapt.
- **[P1] Tags read as buttons and speak jargon.** 24 uppercase keyline chips, with an ink pass on color cells, which is the keyline-button recipe. *Why:* affordance noise at scale and a vocabulary for the secondary persona. *Fix:* on the home page render them as one plain Label line ("React · Next.js · TypeScript") with no box, or cut them from home and keep them on /start-a-project. *Command:* /impeccable distill.
- **[P2] Hierarchy inverted and the hero restates itself.** Hero H1 caps at 4rem while the close H2 reaches 4.5rem; the stream H2s repeat the H1's own lines 500px below it. *Fix:* return the hero to the DESIGN.md display scale, cap the close at or below it, retitle the stream cells "What we build." / "What we draw." so the nav's "What we do" has a true landing. *Command:* /impeccable typeset.
- **[P2] No proof on a Persuade surface.** Nothing is evidence except the handwriting. *Fix:* make the mint cell "Exhibit A: this site": the `<n>` plate plus "The logo, the glyphs and this page were drawn and coded by the same hand." True, specific, free. *Command:* /impeccable bolder.
- **[P3] Phone process strip is dead weight.** Three yellow cells at 300px min-height stack into ~1,500px before the close. *Fix:* below 900px render the steps as one yellow cell with the numbered list. *Command:* /impeccable adapt.

Where the two assessments agree: the detector's 12 off-ramp font sizes are the same per-section clamps the review flagged as the inverted hierarchy (hero 4rem vs close 4.5rem). Where the detector caught what the review missed: the footer legal line length. Where the detector is wrong or moot: the honeypot occlusion, the bone "cream palette" (the brief's stock), the bounce easing that is not in this codebase.

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (first-timer):** "Start a project" vs "Contact" in the hero with no cue; no microcopy about form length or commitment; tag chips in every cell look pressable and say things Jordan cannot evaluate ("MCP servers"); the "&" at the bottom-right of 01.3 and the floating cobalt cube in 02.3 and 02.4 look like layout mistakes.

**Riley (stress tester):** zero work shown, and the only images are synthetic isometric icons that read as stock; three different reply promises ("within two working days", "within a couple of working days", "usually within a few days"); "One person designs and builds it" under a page that says "we" and "us" everywhere; nav "What we do" lands on a cell headed "Lines of code.".

**Casey (distracted mobile):** the page is about 7,100px tall and after the hero the next "Start a project" is around 6,500px with no sticky nav or CTA; the alternating cells and scattered tone colors read as an undifferentiated feed; the mailto button labelled with the full address looks like an input field at 300px wide.

## Minor Observations

- aria-current never matches on the home page; strip hashes or drop it on anchor links.
- No skip link past the nav.
- Two identical coral "Start a project" buttons register-in within 200px of each other in the first viewport.
- The hero glyph scatter means nothing; composed into a legible fragment it would be an in-joke the audience gets.
- The stream lede on cobalt lists exactly the four services that follow as cells.
- Captions ("00 / Lines", "01.1") are decoration pretending to be wayfinding.
- Footer legal hairline is 1px rgba against the 2px ink rule.
- H1 reads to assistive tech as "Lines of code.Lines of ink.Lines by Nick." with no separators.
- The hero reserves a third of its width for glyphs and has no isometric object, so the thin-vs-thick contrast only appears on the second screen.
- Legal paragraph runs ~115 characters per line at wide viewports.

## Questions to Consider
